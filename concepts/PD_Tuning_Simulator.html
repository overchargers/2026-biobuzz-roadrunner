🧠 Understanding PD Control

PD Controller Formula:
Output = (Kp × Error) + (Kd × Rate of Change)
Where Error = Target Angle - Current Angle

🎯 Think of it like balancing a stick on your finger:

Proportional (Kp): The more the stick tilts, the more you move your finger to correct it. If it's leaning far left, you move your finger far right. If it's barely tilted, you make a small correction.

Derivative (Kd): If the stick is falling fast, you need to react quickly and move your finger more. If it's falling slowly, you can make gentler corrections. This prevents overshooting!

Deadband: When the stick is almost perfectly balanced, you stop making tiny adjustments to avoid constant jittering.

🎮 Interactive Tuning Guide:

Step 1: Start with "Oscillating" preset

Click "Oscillating" and start the simulation. Watch the robot shake back and forth - this shows what happens with too much Kp and too little Kd!

Step 2: Add damping

Increase Kd to 0.3-0.5 while keeping Kp high. Watch how the oscillations get smaller and more controlled.

Step 3: Find the sweet spot

Try the "Balanced" preset - this should show smooth, fast alignment without oscillation.

Step 4: Experiment with extremes

Try "Sluggish" (too much damping) vs "Aggressive" (fast but might overshoot) to see the trade-offs.

🔍 What to Watch For:

  • 🔄 Oscillation: Robot shakes back and forth (too much Kp, too little Kd)
  • 🐌 Sluggish: Robot moves very slowly (too much Kd)
  • 🎯 Overshoot: Robot goes past target then comes back (too little Kd)
  • ⚡ Perfect: Fast, smooth approach with minimal overshoot
